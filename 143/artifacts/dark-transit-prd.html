Product requirements · SIH 2026 · SIH26143

Dark Transit

Satellite oil-spill detection, drift hindcasting, and vessel attribution for the National Technical Research Organisation. Most teams will build a slick detector. This one produces a name — and states, on the same page, what that name is not.

01What this is

The source of truth for what Dark Transit does, what it deliberately refuses to do, and how we will know it works. Everything on a slide or in a demo traces back here.

Ships discharge oily bilge water at sea because it is cheaper than paying for reception facilities in port. It is illegal under MARPOL Annex I, and it is close to unenforceable: a satellite finds the slick hours or days later, by which time the vessel is gone and the physical evidence has spread over tens of square kilometres.

The gap is not detection. Detection is a solved research problem with a public labelled dataset. The gap is the step from there is oil here to this hull put it here, and that step is a chain of three inferences — where the oil started, who was there, and whether their behaviour is consistent with having poured it. Dark Transit builds that chain, scores it, and hands an analyst a ranked shortlist with the reasoning attached.

The one line

Everyone else detects the spill. We reconstruct the crime scene and name the suspects — with the limits of that naming printed above the finding, not buried under it.

02The ask, decoded

Official text · SIH26143 · verbatim

“Participants are to design an intelligent automated pipeline to do the following: (a) Detect and characterise the oil spill and calculating geometric properties and age if feasible. (b) Using oceanographic and meteorological data, it is envisaged to trace the slick towards the origin point and time, predict the future flow of the slick, and (c) analyse and attribute the spill to a vessel using historic AIS data to reconstruct vessel traffic around the origin window in space and time. The irrelevant traffic is to be filtered out and potential suspect vessels are to be scored considering various aspects such as proximity, trajectory, behavioural anomalies etc.”

“An automated detection and hindcasting machine learning model … It also ranks potential culprit vessel based on spatio-temporal correlation with AIS data. A suitable visual interface is also to be developed.”

Read past the phrase “machine learning model” and look at where the difficulty actually sits. Only one of the four deliverables is a model-training problem, and that one has a labelled dataset named in the problem statement itself.

03Positioning

An oil-spill detector is a commodity. ESA, EMSA’s CleanSeaNet, and a dozen published papers already do it, and a judge has seen the dark-patch-on-radar slide before. What nobody hands them is the chain of custody from that patch to a hull.

The three claims we make

One — the slick’s shape is a bearing. A discharge made under way lays film along the vessel’s track, so an elongated slick’s principal axis is a heading. Comparing that axis against each candidate’s course over ground is the single most discriminating factor in the model, it is cheap to compute, and it does not depend on the drift reconstruction being right. Most teams will not think of it.

Two — silence is a signal, scored against the vessel’s own baseline. A Class A transponder reports every 2–10 seconds under way. A hull that goes dark on approach to the origin region and resumes after leaving it has produced an anomaly relative to its own cadence — which is the only defensible way to score a gap, because satellite AIS coverage genuinely thins offshore and a naive gap detector would flag half the ocean.

Three — a hull is bright on radar whether or not it broadcasts. The same SAR scene that shows the slick shows ships as point targets. Running a CFAR detector over it and matching the returns against interpolated AIS positions gives a second channel that is independent of the transponder — so a vessel that defeats one is still visible to the other. The problem statement does not ask for this. It is the differentiator.

Where the marks are

Clause (c) is worth more than clause (a) because it is where the problem statement’s own author placed the unsolved part. Build order in §15 reflects that: detection is scheduled fourth, not first, so that a slow model cannot take the demo down with it.

04Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Given one SAR scene and an AIS archive covering the drift horizon, produce a ranked vessel shortlist with a per-factor score breakdown, unattended.
  • Express every spatial and temporal output as a region and a window, never a point and an instant, and show the uncertainty growing as the hindcast runs back.
  • Refuse, explicitly and legibly, when the evidence does not support a conclusion — see §09.
  • Emit a dossier whose first page is the limitations, ahead of the finding.
  • Keep every scoring weight in a versioned JSON pack, editable live in the interface, so a reader can see how much the conclusion depends on any single assumption.

Non-goals

  • Not a real-time monitoring service. One incident, one scene, one window.
  • Not a finding of legal responsibility, and no output may be worded as one.
  • Not an oil-weathering or fate-and-transport model. We advect and diffuse; we do not model evaporation, emulsification or biodegradation beyond what OpenDrift gives us for free.
  • Not a global AIS ingest. One region, one archive, one 72-hour window.
  • Not a spoofing detector. We note that AIS can be forged and score kinematic plausibility; we do not claim to catch a careful spoof.

06Architecture

Seven stages, strictly sequential, three of which can halt the run. Each stage writes a typed artefact to the run directory, so any stage can be replayed against a stored input without re-running the ones before it — which is also what makes the demo robust.

DT — Detection

Oil damps short capillary and gravity waves, which suppresses Bragg scattering, so a slick returns almost nothing and arrives as a dark region. So does a low-wind cell, a biogenic film, an algal bloom, rain, and the lee of a headland. Separating them is the whole of this stage.

Stated limitation — carry this to the slide

Forward advection with diffusion is well-posed. Running it backward is not: diffusion cannot be un-mixed, so the backward cloud is a reachable set — the region a particle could have come from — and not a probability density over true origins. We will use the phrase “origin region”, never “origin point”, and never quote a probability for a specific launch position.

08The attribution model

Five factors, each normalised to [0,1], combined as a weighted mean. The weights normalise on read, so a reader can move one without having to rebalance the rest.

// weights.v4.json — the only place these live
score(v) = Σᵢ wᵢ · fᵢ(v) ⁄ Σᵢ wᵢ

// default pack
containment 22   timing 18   heading 26   broadcast 24   class 10
The five factors
FactorComputed fromWhy it discriminates
ContainmentOverlap fraction of the vessel’s track — or its reachable set across a gap — with the origin region.Direct spatial evidence. Weak on its own, because the region is large by construction.
TimingAlignment of the crossing, or the dark period, with the hindcast origin window.Cheaply clears vessels that transited hours before the window opened.
Heading|COG − slick principal axis|, modulo 180°.Strongest single factor. A discharge under way lays film along the track; a 40° mismatch is decisive and does not depend on the drift being right.
BroadcastGap duration and placement, scored against the vessel’s own median cadence.Behavioural rather than geometric, so it is genuinely independent of the other four.
Class priorShip type and dimensions from AIS static data.Weakest, and weighted accordingly. A 27 m fishing hull cannot produce a 12 km² slick.

The reachable set across a broadcast gap

This is the part that does real work. When a transponder goes dark, the vessel does not vanish — it becomes bounded. From the last fix it can travel no further than its plausible maximum speed allows; from the next fix, the same constraint runs backward. The intersection of those two discs, swept over the dark period, is the region it could have occupied.

Why the weights are exposed

Any weighting is an assumption, and hiding it invites exactly the question we cannot answer. Putting the sliders in the interface converts that weakness into the demonstration: drop heading to zero in front of a judge and watch the leader margin collapse. That is an honest statement about how much of the conclusion rests on one geometric argument, and it is worth more than a defended number.

09Gates: when the system refuses

The product’s central claim is that it knows what it cannot establish. That claim is only credible if the refusals are implemented, reachable, and demonstrated. Each gate below halts the run, states its reason in the log and the dossier, and is worth showing deliberately in the demo.

Gate 1 · detection confidence

Below 0.50 confidence, report a look-alike and stop.

No origin region is computed and no vessel is named. The output is “a dark feature was found and we do not believe it is oil”, with the rejection criteria that drove that.

Gate 2 · origin region size

Past a 36 km 95 % containment radius, drop the spatial filter.

Beyond roughly 36 hours of hindcast the region is large enough that it no longer constrains traffic — nearly every vessel in the area intersects it. The pipeline abandons the spatial filter at that point and reports attribution as unavailable, rather than shrinking the region to look decisive.

Gate 3 · dark channel availability

No radar pass inside the origin window, no second channel.

The dark channel is declared unavailable in those words. A single-channel result is still reported, flagged as single-channel.

Gate 4 · leader margin

If rank 1 and rank 2 differ by less than 0.10, name neither.

The system reports that the evidence does not separate the candidates and returns both. This is the gate most likely to fire on real data, and the one most worth building.

Gate 5 · archive coverage

If the AIS archive does not span the drift horizon, do not start.

Caught in pre-flight (IN-4), before any compute is spent. A partial archive silently truncates the candidate set, which is worse than not running.

Rejected alternatives — say these out loud on the slide

  • Not ArcGIS / ENVI. Licensed, and undeployable to the operational users named in §05.
  • Not a from-scratch segmentation architecture. The dataset is small and the recipe is public; inventing here spends a week to lose accuracy.
  • Not YOLO on the slick. Object detection returns a box. The task needs a polygon, because the polygon’s principal axis is the strongest scoring factor.
  • Not a learned end-to-end attribution model. There is no labelled corpus of attributed spills to train it on, and a black box cannot produce the per-factor justification that makes the dossier usable.

13Non-functional requirements & dual-use safety

This system points at a named vessel. That is the whole value and the whole hazard, and the safeguards below are product requirements, not a disclaimer appended at the end.

IDRequirement
NFR-1Explainability. Every score carries its factors, their weights, a written justification per factor, and the weight-pack version. No bare numbers anywhere in the product.
NFR-2Ranking, not finding. Output is a shortlist for investigation. Generated text may not assert responsibility; RP-3 makes this checkable.
NFR-3Absence is not evidence. Vessels below the AIS carriage threshold broadcast nothing. Their absence from the analysis is stated as a limitation and never scored against anyone.
NFR-4No automated transmission. The dossier is generated; sending it to an enforcement body is a human action, with a human name attached.
NFR-5Reproducibility. A run is fully described by scene ID, archive, forcing sources, parameters and weight-pack version. Same inputs, same output.
NFR-6Uncertainty is first-class. No spatial output renders as a point, no temporal output as an instant.
NFR-7Provenance of traffic. Real versus synthetic AIS is declared in the run log, in the interface, and on dossier page 1.
NFR-8Performance. A full run over one scene and a 72 h archive completes in under 10 minutes on a laptop, so the demo is live rather than a recording.

The ablation

Re-score the synthetic evaluation set with each factor zeroed in turn, and report top-1 recall for each. This produces the sensitivity claim in §08 as a measurement rather than an assertion, and it is the single most useful table we can put in front of a technical judge.

The negative control

Run the full pipeline on a clean scene with no spill and ordinary traffic. The correct output is Gate 1 firing. A system that names a vessel here is worse than useless, and this test runs before every demo.

15Build order

Each step must stand alone and demo alone before the next connects to it.

  1. Scene on a map. Load a Sentinel-1 GRD, calibrate, render in MapLibre. Nothing else.
  2. Hand-drawn polygon → drift → origin region. Hardcode the slick. Get the forcing data flowing and the uncertainty growing on screen. Detection is not involved.
  3. Synthetic AIS → filter → score → ranked list. The whole of clause (c), against a hardcoded polygon and a real origin region.
  4. Train the detector and swap out the hardcoded polygon.
  5. Dark channel — CFAR and AIS matching.
  6. Dossier generation.
  7. Gates, adversarial scenes, ablation, negative control.
  8. Polish — time slider, weight sliders, run log.
Why detection is fourth

It is the only machine-learning component and the only one that can silently consume a week. Everything downstream is buildable and demonstrable against a hand-drawn polygon. If detection underperforms, the system still runs, still ranks, and still demos — and clause (c), which is where the marks are, is unaffected.
