Spill attribution assessment

A 12.4 km² slick was detected in the Gulf of Kutch approaches on 14 March 2026 at 06:12 UTC. Backward drift places its origin in a region 41 km west-southwest of the detection, between 22:00 and 02:00 UTC. One vessel of nine in that window ceased broadcasting on approach and resumed after departure.

What this assessment cannot establish

  1. A reachable set establishes that a vessel could have been at the origin. It never establishes that it was.
  2. Silence has innocent causes. Equipment fails and satellite coverage thins offshore; the gap here is scored against this vessel's own cadence, not against zero.
  3. A transponder can broadcast a false position. Kinematic checks catch clumsy spoofing, not careful spoofing.
  4. Vessels below the carriage threshold broadcast nothing at all, so their absence from this analysis is not evidence of anything.
  5. Drift error compounds hourly. Beyond 36 hours the origin region stops constraining traffic and this method should not be relied on.
  6. Traffic in this assessment is synthetic. Real AIS for these waters was not available.

Finding

MV Kestrel Ridge, a crude tanker of 118 m, is ranked first at a composite score computed from five weighted factors. Two independent channels place it in the origin region: a dead-reckoned reachable set spanning its 2 h 40 m of silence overlaps the region by 71 %, and an unmatched radar return of comparable estimated length sits inside that same envelope at 23:41 UTC.

The strongest single factor is geometric. The slick's principal axis runs 041° true; the vessel's course through the region was 047°. A discharge made under way lays film along the track, and a six-degree agreement is difficult to produce by coincidence.

Recommended action

Boarding inspection at next port call, with sampling of slop tank contents for comparison against a slick sample if one can still be recovered. This document ranks vessels for investigation. It is not a finding of responsibility and should not be represented as one.
